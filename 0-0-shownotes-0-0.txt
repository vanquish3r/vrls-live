National Day of Remembrance and Action on Violence Against Women

Tonight I want to pause the jokes and the chaos for a few minutes and talk about something that matters. Today is the National Day of Remembrance and Action on Violence Against Women in Canada. It is a day that asks us to slow down, pay attention, and acknowledge the reality that far too many women have lived through, and in many cases, never survived.

Most of us heard the story of the École Polytechnique tragedy when we were younger, usually in a textbook or a quick class discussion. Back then it was easy to treat it like a distant event. Fourteen women killed simply because they were women. As you get older, that hits you differently. You start to understand the weight of it. You realise these were students with dreams, plans, families, jokes they never got to tell, futures that were stolen. It is not just history. It is a reminder of what hatred and fear can grow into when no one stops it.

---

But today is not only about looking back. It is also about looking around. Violence against women is not some old problem we solved years ago. It still shows up in homes, workplaces, friend groups, relationships, and online spaces. It shows up quietly in small comments that chip away at someone’s confidence. It shows up loudly in situations that turn dangerous. And it affects people we know, even if we are not always aware of it. Someone you talk to every day might be carrying something heavy and hiding it because they feel they have to.

This day is not meant to make anyone feel guilty. It is meant to open our eyes. It is a reminder that we all have some power to make things better. Not by being perfect or heroic, but by being present. By paying attention when something feels off. By believing someone who finally speaks up. By refusing to laugh along when someone makes a joke that crosses a line. By choosing to be someone who makes their corner of the world, including VR, safer and more supportive.

---

There is a quote that fits today perfectly. It says, not every man, but every woman has a story. And that is the part we cannot ignore. Because behind that idea is a truth that should never sit comfortably with any of us. Every woman has a moment she remembers. A situation that made her feel unsafe. A comment that crossed a line. A time she was dismissed, ignored, or blamed for something that was never her fault. When you hear that quote and really sit with it, you realise this is not a rare experience. It is woven into daily life. And that is exactly why days like this exist.

Everyone deserves to live without fear. Everyone deserves safety in their own home, their own relationships, and their own communities. And the truth is that safety grows from awareness. It grows from people deciding to care. It grows from those small choices that seem like nothing but actually matter more than we realise.

---

So as we keep the show rolling tonight, I want us to carry a little bit of this with us. Remember the women whose lives were taken. Remember the people who survived violence and are still trying to rebuild. Remember that the impact of these events does not disappear just because time moves on. Honour their stories by choosing awareness, compassion, and action in your own life.

Tonight we laugh and have fun, but we can also make space for something real. And maybe that combination is exactly what community is meant to look like.

Darwin Award

Tonight’s near miss comes from the depths of winter, when everyone thinks they have a shortcut to beat the snow. Back in December 2008, in New Bedford, Massachusetts, one man decided he was done shovelling. Tired of bending over, tired of freezing, he came up with a plan that he probably thought was brilliant at the time. Instead of picking up a shovel like a normal human, he grabbed a propane blowtorch. His goal was simple. Melt the snow and ice on his back porch. Easy, right?

Well, here’s the part where it goes terribly wrong. He aimed the torch at the porch, and almost immediately, the flame caught the siding. Vinyl siding melts fast, and the fire quickly spread up the outside of the building. This wasn’t just a small porch fire. It raced up three stories of the house, threatening the upstairs apartments and the wooden framing of the building. It was chaos, with smoke filling the air and the kind of fire that makes you stop and think about every life choice you’ve ever made.

---

Firefighters arrived and worked to control the blaze, but the damage was already done. Bedrooms upstairs, structural elements, wiring inside the walls, all caught in the mess. In the end, it cost about thirty thousand dollars to repair, and that is just the financial damage. Can you imagine the stress, the panic, and the embarrassment?

The craziest part is that nobody was hurt. No one was burned, no one trapped, just a whole lot of property damage and a very memorable lesson. Fire officials later warned that the safest way to deal with snow and ice is the old-fashioned way. Grab a shovel, bundle up, and take your time. There is no shortcut that involves fire and propane.

---

This story is a perfect reminder that in winter, Mother Nature always wins, and sometimes your attempts to cheat her can end with flames instead of cleared snow. You might walk away with a story, you might walk away with scars, or, if you’re lucky, like this man, you walk away with nothing but a very expensive lesson in what not to do.
