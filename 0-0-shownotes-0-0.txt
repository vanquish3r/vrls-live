TIL Against My Will

It's time for Today I Learned Against My Will… the segment where I find facts that humanity absolutely could have survived without, but now we can't, because I had access to the internet and poor decision making skills.

Tonight's theme is cursed facts. These are not fun facts. Nobody is having fun. These are facts that make you slightly uncomfortable in ways you can't explain to HR.

And as always… if I have to live with this knowledge… I'm taking you all down with me.

Fact 1 – Dust

Today I learned that a lot of the dust in your house is made of your own dead skin. So when someone says wow it's dusty in here they're not insulting your cleaning… They're saying congratulations on existing aggressively. You're not dirty. You're just… slowly disintegrating like a dramatic movie villain. Cleaning isn't cleaning. It's just you going: "I would like to remove the evidence that I used to be younger."

Fact 2 – Wet bones

Today I learned your bones are wet. Nobody likes that sentence. Nobody has ever enjoyed hearing those four words together. I hate this. You hate this. We all hate this. Nobody has ever heard your bones are wet and said oh cool thanks for sharing. You are a soggy skeleton driving a meat vehicle fueled by caffeine and unresolved childhood issues. And if that description doesn't make humans sound fake, nothing will.

---
Fact 3 – Your brain named itself

Today I learned your brain named itself. Think about that too long and you start to feel like you shouldn't be allowed to operate heavy machinery. Your brain literally went: "I will call myself the brain. I am in charge now." That's like writing your own job title and promoting yourself to CEO at the same time. No vote. No oversight. No committee. Just raw confidence. Honestly the brain has the same energy as someone who gets promoted because they were the only one who showed up.

Fact 4 – Eyelash mites

Today I learned there are microscopic mites living in your eyelashes. They come out at night. They walk around your face. They have families. They probably have little mite drama. You are not a person. You are a neighbourhood. Somewhere on your face there's probably a mite saying: "Property values are really going up on the left cheek."

Fact 5 – Stomach self-destruct prevention

Today I learned your stomach constantly replaces its lining so it doesn't digest itself. Your body wakes up every day and goes: "Okay step one… don't eat ourselves today." It literally has a daily maintenance checklist like:

*Breathe
*Circulate blood
*Don't eat yourself again

Honestly some days just accomplishing don't eat yourself feels like enough.

---
Fact 6 – Tongue awareness attack

Today I learned your tongue never rests comfortably once you think about it. Right now you're thinking: Where is it supposed to go? Is it touching my teeth? Is it too flat? Am I doing tongue wrong? I've just turned a fully automatic system into manual mode. You were breathing and tonguing just fine a minute ago. Now you're driving stick shift.

Fact 7 – Humans glow

Today I learned humans actually glow slightly from chemical reactions in our bodies. We just can't see it. So technically you're glowing. Just not in a helpful way. You're not glowing like a Disney princess. You're glowing like a dying phone battery.
"Wow you're radiant."
"Yeah but only scientifically and it's not improving my situation."

Fact 8 – Teeth are outside bones

Today I learned your teeth are technically outside bones. They're part of your skeleton… but exposed. Meaning your skeleton is sticking out of your face at all times. And we just accepted that as normal. Evolution really looked at that design and said: "Yeah let's just leave these bones out in the open. What could go wrong?" Dentists are basically skeleton mechanics.

---
Fact 9 – You are mostly bacteria

Today I learned that your body contains roughly as many bacterial cells as human cells. So technically… you're not even fully you. You're basically a 50/50 partnership and you didn't even sign the contract. You're less of a person and more of a biological timeshare. You wake up thinking you're in charge, but really you're just the property manager of trillions of microscopic freeloaders.

Fact 10 – Your brain edits reality

Today I learned your brain filters out constant sensations like your clothes touching you so you don't go insane. Meaning right now your brain is deciding what parts of reality you are allowed to experience. Your brain is basically running spam filters on existence. "You don't need to feel your socks. That's junk mail." Which means your brain is basically your personal reality moderator. And honestly mine is doing a questionable job.

And that's Today I Learned Against My Will… the segment that proves knowledge is not always power… sometimes it's just emotional damage. Join us next time when I discover more facts that make me want to return my body for store credit. We'll be right back after I try to forget everything I just told you and you try to figure out what your tongue is doing again.

History, But Petty

This is History… But Make It Petty. The segment where we look at historical events and realize world history was basically shaped by people who absolutely could not let things go. We’re taught history is about wars, empires, and great leaders… but honestly? A lot of it is just hurt feelings with resources. Tonight we're looking at moments that prove humanity has always been dramatic… they just didn't have Twitter yet.

1 – The cornflakes anti-fun campaign

John Harvey Kellogg invented cornflakes because he believed bland food would stop people from… entertaining themselves." Yes. Cornflakes exist because one man thought flavour led to moral collapse. Imagine being so against fun you decide breakfast needs to taste like cardboard. "People are enjoying themselves too much. We must intervene with dry flakes." And now we just eat them like this isn't the pettiest origin story for a cereal imaginable.

2 – Napoleon: victim of the first smear campaign

Napoleon wasn't actually unusually short. The British just kept calling him short because they hated him. That's it. That's the origin of the Napoleon complex. History's longest running insult came from professional level trash talk. This was basically the 1800s version of: "I'm losing the war but I'm still roasting you." And it worked. Two hundred years later people still believe it. That is generational hating.

3 – Australia lost a war to emus

In 1932 Australia declared war on emus because they were destroying crops. They sent soldiers. Soldiers With machine guns. Against birds. And the birds won because they ran too fast and wouldn't stand still like polite enemies. Imagine being the general having to report: "Sir… the birds are using guerrilla tactics." The emus didn't even know they were in a war. They were just out there existing aggressively.

---
4 – Math argument escalates to sword fight

Scientist Tycho Brahe lost part of his nose in a duel over a disagreement about math. Math. Not politics. Not love. Not money. Numbers. Imagine arguing over equations and immediately jumping to: "I will defend my spreadsheet with violence." He then wore a metal nose for the rest of his life instead of admitting maybe he overreacted. That is some commitment to being wrong.

5 – The world's pettiest revenge chair

A man named Thomas Blood tried to steal the Crown Jewels of England and instead of executing him, the king was so impressed he gave him land. Imagine attempting one of the biggest robberies in history… getting caught… and the king basically going: "You know what? I respect the hustle." This man literally bonked the Crown Jewels' keeper on the head, tried to escape, got captured… and somehow turned that into a networking opportunity. That's not crime. That's career advancement. This is the historical version of failing upward.

6 – Lord Byron kept a pet bear because rules said no dogs

At Cambridge University students weren't allowed to have dogs in their dorms. So poet Lord Byron got a pet bear. Because technically the rules didn't say anything about bears. Imagine being the administration trying to enforce rules. "Sir you cannot have a dog." "This is not a dog." That's not rebellion. That's malicious compliance at expert level.

---
7 – The emperor who hated pants

Roman Emperor Caligula once declared war on the sea and had his soldiers stab the water and collect seashells as 'spoils of war.' Imagine being a soldier just standing there thinking: "I joined the army for this?" "Attack the ocean!" How do you even lose morale after that?

8 – The longest grudge ever recorded

There were two rival opera singers in the 1700s who hated each other so much that when one died, the other refused to sing at the memorial and instead performed somewhere else out of spite. Imagine being so petty you schedule competing grief. "I will mourn… but competitively."

9 – The scientist who named things after himself constantly

Isaac Newton once got into such a bad rivalry with another scientist that he basically used his influence to erase the guy's contributions from history. Newton didn't just discover gravity. He also discovered academic pettiness. 
This is the intellectual version of editing someone out of the group photo.

---
10 – The world's pettiest silence

President John Adams and Thomas Jefferson were such rivals they stopped speaking to each other for over a decade after being friends. Ten years. No reconciliation. Just pure I am not talking to you energy. Imagine being that committed to being mad. And yes… they both died on the same day years later which feels like history going: "Okay that's enough drama from you two."
