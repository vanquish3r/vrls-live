Darwin Award

So this happened in Kingston, Ontario in 2018 near Queen’s University, where a St. Patrick’s Day party was in full swing. Like most St. Patrick’s parties, it started normally enough and then slowly evolved into what can only be described as a live demonstration of why common sense sometimes leaves the building before the guests do. At some point, and I really want you to imagine how this suggestion got approved, someone decided the party would be better on the roof. Not near the roof. Not looking at the roof. On the roof. And this was not two people sneaking up there. About 40 people climbed onto the roof of this house. Forty. That is not a party anymore. That is a structural engineering stress test.

Nobody up there was thinking about weight limits. Nobody was doing math. Nobody was saying maybe this old student rental house was not designed to support what is essentially a rooftop concert. No, they were celebrating, cheering, probably yelling things like “this is the best St. Patrick’s ever” while the house underneath them was basically begging for mercy. Then came the moment everyone except them could see coming. Someone heard cracking. Not music. Not cheering. Cracking. And like in every bad decision story, someone probably said, “it’s probably fine.”

It was not fine.
---
The roof gave out and about 40 people dropped straight through into the house like the world’s worst surprise entrance. Imagine just standing inside holding a drink and suddenly the ceiling becomes people. That is not how architecture is supposed to work. And here is the unbelievable part. Nobody died. Nobody even had life threatening injuries. One person had a scraped leg. That is it. Forty people fall through a roof and the worst outcome is basically someone needing a bandaid and a long moment of self reflection.

That is pure honorable mention Darwin energy because this could have gone very differently. Broken bones, head injuries, real tragedy. Instead they all just got the most aggressive reality check possible. And you know somewhere after it happened there was definitely at least one guy brushing insulation off his shoulders going, “so… afterparty?”

Honestly, that might be the most St. Patrick’s Day part of the whole story.
---
So the lesson here is simple. If you are ever at a party and someone suggests everyone should climb onto the roof, that is not a fun idea. That is a test of your survival instincts. Roofs are not dance floors. Roofs are not patios. Roofs are not emotional support structures. Roofs have one job and it is not holding 40 drunk university students.
