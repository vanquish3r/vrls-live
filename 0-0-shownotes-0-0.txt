Chaos Chain

Start with a simple VR problem, other host adds an absurd consequence, cannot undo or solve the problem - have to make it worse. Static will then escalate it further and then random audience members are picked to continue it. When it goes off the rails, we start over.

RULES: 
You cannot solve the problem

You cannot say “and then it stops”

The goal is escalation, not logic

-I log into VR and my hands are missing.
-I load in and my face is on the back of my head.
-I accidentally teleport into a private conversation.
-Stairs only work going up, not down.
-The environment reacts emotionally to me.
-The world reloads every time someone joins.

Darwin Award

Ah yes, New Year’s resolutions. That magical time of year when people look at their lives, sigh deeply, and say, “This year will be different,” before immediately doing something profoundly stupid.

So this guy decides that this is the year. The year he’s going to get fit. No more excuses. No easing into it. No gentle walks. No stretching. No, he wakes up on January 1st with the confidence of someone who watched one motivational video and immediately believed they were invincible.

He laces up brand new running shoes. Still pristine. Still optimistic. Still unaware they’re about to witness a crime.

He heads outside to go for his very first run in years. Possibly decades. And instead of choosing a track, a trail, or literally anywhere designed for humans to jog safely, he decides to run along a busy road. Because why not add traffic to the mix when your lungs already feel like they’re on fire?

About five minutes in, his body begins to file formal complaints. His breathing is chaotic. His legs feel like boiled noodles. His heart is doing something that feels legally questionable. But quitting would mean admitting defeat, and it’s January 1st. You cannot quit on January 1st. That’s illegal. That’s against the spirit of the holiday.

So he pushes himself harder.

And this is where it goes beautifully wrong.

---

He becomes lightheaded. Dizzy. The world tilts. He stumbles off balance and very nearly runs directly into traffic. Like, close enough that drivers had to slam on their brakes and collectively wonder what kind of unhinged resolution-based madness they were witnessing.

He does not die. He does not get hit. He does not earn a full Darwin Award.

But he does collapse onto the grass, gasping, reassessing every life choice he’s ever made, while strangers stare and silently judge him for trying to transform his life too aggressively.

Paramedics are called. Not because he’s dying, but because everyone involved needs reassurance that he hasn’t completely lost his mind. Turns out he was dehydrated, wildly out of shape, and tried to go from couch to cardio god in under ten minutes.

---

The diagnosis? “Please don’t do that again.”

And that is why this man earns an honourable mention in the Darwin Awards of New Year’s resolutions. Not because he didn’t want to improve himself. Good for him. Growth is great. But because he believed January 1st meant his body would immediately forgive years of inactivity and reward him with sudden athletic ability.

So remember this story when you’re setting your resolutions. Start slow. Drink water. Maybe don’t sprint next to traffic on day one. Self-improvement is a journey, not a near-death experience.
