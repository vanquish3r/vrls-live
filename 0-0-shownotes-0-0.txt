Valentine's Day with kitty

Alright. Let’s talk about Valentine’s Day.

Because every year around February 14th we all just collectively agree that this is the day of love. Hearts. Roses. Chocolate. Soft music. Overpriced prix fixe menus where you sit elbow to elbow with twelve other couples pretending this is intimate.

But when you actually look at the origin story, it’s less “aww” and more “are you serious right now?”

So first of all, the most popular story tied to Valentine’s Day is the whole Saint Valentine situation. And we don’t even really know which Valentine it was. There were multiple. Several men named Valentine were executed by the Romans. Executed. That’s the vibe.

One version says he was secretly marrying couples after Emperor Claudius II decided single men made better soldiers and banned marriage. Which already sounds like a villain origin story. So Valentine’s out there doing underground weddings like some kind of romantic outlaw… and then he gets arrested and beheaded on February 14th.

Beheaded.

And at some point humanity went, “You know what this needs? Heart-shaped chocolates.”

Another version says he healed a jailer’s daughter and signed a letter “from your Valentine” before his execution. Which is sweet in a tragic way. But again. The foundation of this holiday is execution. It’s not candles and violins. It’s Rome saying, “Off with his head.”

---
How did we go from public execution to stuffed bears holding little satin hearts?

And then there’s Lupercalia. This ancient Roman festival that happened in mid February. It involved animal sacrifice. Goats. Sometimes a dog. Priests would cut the hides into strips and then run around slapping women with them because apparently that was supposed to promote fertility.

Imagine explaining that at dinner.

“Oh yeah babe, Valentine’s Day started with ritual sacrifice and ceremonial whipping.”

Are you serious.

And then the Church comes along and goes, “You know what, let’s Christianize this whole chaotic goat festival situation.” So now we’ve got a martyred saint layered over a fertility ritual involving blood and hides. Romantic. Sooooooooo romantic.

And that’s before we even get to the medieval era where people believed February 14th was the day birds started mating. Which is somehow both wholesome and deeply awkward.

Like, congratulations everyone. We based our love holiday on birds hooking up.

Then fast forward to the 1700s and 1800s when people start sending handwritten notes. Cute. Charming. Very Jane Austen coded. That I can respect. Emma, Mansfield Park, Northanger Abbey, Sense and Sensibility. She knew romance.

But then capitalism shows up and says, “What if we monetized emotional validation?”
---

Enter mass-produced Valentine’s cards in the 19th century. Enter heart-shaped candy boxes in the early 1900s. Enter jewellery ads telling you that if you really love someone, you’d better prove it with something shiny that comes with a receipt.

Now it’s a retail holiday.

And not a subtle one either. You walk into any store on January 2nd and it’s already screaming at you in red and pink. The Christmas decorations aren’t even cold yet and boom. Giant inflatable teddy bear holding a heart that says “Be Mine.”

Be mine or what? Are we threatening people now?

And the pressure. Oh my god, the pressure.

If you’re single, you’re supposed to feel like you’re missing something. If you’re in a relationship, you’re supposed to perform. Book the dinner. Buy the gift. Post the photo. Prove publicly that your love is thriving.

It’s less about connection and more about optics.

And if you don’t participate? Suddenly you’re the Grinch of romance. “Oh you don’t like Valentine’s Day?” No Karen, I just don’t think I need a prix fixe steak to confirm my emotional stability.

And can we talk about how aggressive the marketing is? Ads that basically imply if you don’t buy the necklace, you don’t care. If you don’t get the flowers, you’re neglectful. If you don’t plan something elaborate, your relationship is hanging by a thread.

---
Meanwhile the original guy this is named after literally got executed - potentially for marrying soldiers and their beloveds - and probably did not foresee a future where we’d measure love by the size of a chocolate box.

Also, who decided everything had to be red? Red roses. Red hearts. Red lingerie. Red velvet cake. It’s like the entire aesthetic is “mildly alarming.”

We’ve taken something rooted in martyrdom and fertility rituals and bird mating and turned it into a glittery capitalist endurance test.

And the irony is, love is actually simple most of the time. It’s showing up. It’s listening. It’s not secretly marrying people under imperial law while dodging execution.

But every February we crank it up to eleven and pretend that this one day defines everything.

So yeah. When you really think about it, Valentine’s Day is kind of morbid. Kind of weird. Historically chaotic. Commercially aggressive.

And somehow we all just roll with it. “Happy Beheading Anniversary, babe. I got you chocolates.”

Are you serious?

Anyway, if you celebrate it, have fun. If you don’t, also valid. Just remember the next time you see a heart-shaped display and a sign telling you love costs $89.99 plus tax… this whole thing started with goats, birds, and a Roman execution.

Romance.
(Don't worry babe, we'll abstain but still get sushi!)

Darwin Award

Alright, strap in, because this is a love story that goes exactly where it should not go. This is an honorable mention Darwin Award story, which means nobody died, but you will spend the next few minutes mentally facepalming.

So we’ve got a young couple. You know the type. Head over heels, Instagram-ready lovebirds, probably thinking they invented romance. They decide the perfect date is a picnic on a frozen lake. Yes, you heard that correctly. A frozen lake. At night. In winter.

Now, most people would think, “Hey, maybe we should sit on a bench, or have cocoa indoors.” But not these geniuses. They drive onto the ice, roll down the windows for that “crisp, romantic winter air,” and probably toast each other with some hot chocolate, thinking they are living a Hallmark movie.

Meanwhile, the ice is beneath them, and it’s silently plotting. It’s creaking, groaning, sending out little spider cracks like, I see you. I know what you’re doing. You’re going to regret this. But our couple? They are too busy whispering things like “I love you more than anything” and “this is magical” to notice physics exists.

So far, so romantic. But here’s where it gets cinematic. The ice begins to tilt. Slowly. Just enough that their car starts to slide toward the middle of the lake. And that’s when panic sets in. Not from the ice breaking, no, panic from realizing their romantic, poetic moment might turn into a Darwin Award entry.

They scramble. They slip. They flail. And I mean flail like humans are not designed to slide gracefully on thin ice. It’s equal parts terror, equal parts slapstick comedy, and 100 percent them wondering how they got here. The audience is imagining: “Is this like Titanic but less music and more stupidity?”
---
And then—miracle—somehow they make it out. Alive. Shivering. Probably swearing at each other. And the ice? The ice is just sitting there like a passive-aggressive mother, silently judging their life choices.

Now, imagine telling this story to anyone later. “So, we thought a frozen lake would be romantic.” Pause. “And then it almost killed us.” Pause. “But we lived.” Yeah, nothing says romance like a near-death experience with frostbite as your chaperone.

Let’s break it down. Why did this happen?

First, young love makes you temporarily immune to common sense. Check.

Second, moonlight makes you think you’re in a romantic painting, not a physics experiment. Check.

Third, apparently, ice is optional until it breaks. Check.

And you know what’s perfect for the Late Show? The mental image. Two people, heads together, lips puckered, mid-swoon, completely oblivious to the fact that below them is a frozen sheet that has every intention of giving them a Darwin moment. Audience sees it. They gasp. They laugh. They cringe. Exactly what you want.

After they finally get out, probably cold, bruised, humiliated, they probably decide maybe next time: a café, a warm blanket, literally anywhere that doesn’t want to kill you. And we, the audience, are left with the priceless lesson: Love is great. Frozen lakes are not.
---
But here’s the cherry on top. The universe? Still hilarious. These two survived, yes. But the story lives forever. And that is what honorable mentions are all about. You did something absurd, stupid, and near-death, and you walked away. You win the Darwin bronze medal for sheer audacity.

So, audience, remember this if you’re ever plotting your own winter romance. A frozen lake is not an accessory. Hypothermia is not part of the date package. Physics does not bend for love. And the Darwin Awards? They are always watching, taking notes, and quietly laughing at all of us.
