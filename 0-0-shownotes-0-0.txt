This Week's Bright Spot

This week’s Bright Spot goes to 5DollarChips.

Now, I need everyone to picture this for a second.

There are thrill rides… and then there are those slingshot rides. The ones that look less like amusement park attractions and more like something invented after someone asked, “What if we launched a human into orbit, but politely?”

And 5DollarChips looked at this absolutely unhinged machine… sat down… got strapped in… and said, “Yeah. Let’s do this.”

Which honestly already tells you something important.

Because most of us? We watch those rides from the ground. We hold someone’s bag. We film. We offer emotional support from a very safe distance while silently thinking, not today, Satan.

But he got on anyway.

And sure, it’s easy to laugh at the screaming, the chaos, the pure existential regret that hits about half a second after launch. But underneath all of that is something really genuine.

That kind of moment takes courage.

Not the movie kind of courage. Not the dramatic hero speech kind. Real courage. The kind where your heart is racing, your brain is questioning every decision that led you there, and you still choose to go forward.
---
And the reason this feels like such a perfect Bright Spot isn’t just about a wild ride.

It’s about what it represents.

Because that willingness to strap in and face something intense head on? That’s the same strength it takes to face your own struggles. To look your issues in the eye instead of avoiding them. To confront the uncomfortable stuff, the scary stuff, the things that make you doubt yourself.

And 5DollarChips has shown, again and again, that he has that ability.

He doesn’t run from challenges forever. He faces them. He works through them. He keeps showing up, even when things feel overwhelming.

That’s bravery too.

Sometimes the hardest battles aren’t loud or visible. They’re internal. They’re the moments where you have to believe you’re capable before you actually feel capable.

And I think what’s important here is this.

You’re stronger than you give yourself credit for.

You have the ability to take on hard things, to push past fear, to conquer what’s in front of you when you decide it matters. That slingshot ride is just a very dramatic reminder of something that’s already true.
---
When you set your mind to something, you do it.

You rise to it.

You survive it.

And you come out the other side laughing, growing, and maybe a little shocked at what you were capable of all along.

So this Bright Spot is also a reminder, not just for you but for anyone watching tonight.

Give yourself grace.

Recognize your courage.

Have confidence in the person you already are, because bravery isn’t something you’re trying to become. It’s something you’ve been demonstrating all along.

Sometimes courage looks like personal growth.

Sometimes it looks like facing your demons.

And sometimes… it looks like being launched screaming into the sky and proving to yourself that you can handle more than you ever thought possible.

5DollarChips, this week’s Bright Spot is yours.

Darwin Award

Alright, everyone, this one is a winter near miss that actually happened, and it checks all the boxes. It is cold, it is stupid, it is hilarious, and every time I read it I imagine the universe slowly sighing and facepalming.

So this story comes out of Minnesota, because of course it does. They get cold winters that make the rest of us question whether warmth was ever a good idea in the first place.

Now picture a man named Keith, normal guy, decent taste in flannel, and convinced that winter is just a social construct. One morning he wakes up, steps outside, and looks at his driveway buried under eight inches of fresh snow. And like all reasonable humans, he thinks, “No thanks. Not today.”

But instead of getting the snowblower, a shovel, or literally anything that requires effort, he makes a genius life choice.

He goes to the garage, pulls out a leaf blower, because of course. Not a snowblower. Not a shovel. A leaf blower. The thing you use in autumn when you accidentally plant pumpkins and then regret all of your life choices.

He wants to blast the snow off the driveway with the leaf blower, which is about as effective as asking a kitten to do your taxes. But Keith is committed. He starts up the leaf blower and goes to town, face down in snow that does not care about leaf blowers. Snow doesn’t care about logic. Snow doesn’t care about emotions. Snow just laughs quietly in the background.
---
So he’s standing there blasting 20 pounds of snow with 5 pounds of leaf blower, and suddenly the machine gloms up. It gets stuck. It jams. And instead of turning it off like a sane person, Keith decides to lean forward and poke at it with his hand while the blower is still running.

Yes, that happened. That was his plan.

Now here’s where this turns into a winter near miss and not a Darwin Award fatality. As Keith is poking at the still‑running leaf blower full of snow, it jerks, flings snow back at him, and knocks him flat on his back in the driveway. He lands looking exactly like a cartoon character who has just forgotten all of his life regret.

He’s sprawled there, leaf blower on his chest, snow in his beard, and he’s perfectly fine. Nothing broken, nothing crushed, just dazed and very cold, and absolutely aware that his plan was not brilliant.

At that moment a neighbour walks by, takes one look at Keith flopped on the snow like a confused walrus, and says, with zero judgement but maximum clarity, “Dude. You could have just shoveled.”

And that, my friends, is the moment Keith realized something that should have been obvious before he started wielding a leaf blower like a medieval weapon: winter does not respect shortcuts.
---
He survived, he walked away, and he now refers to that moment as “The Great Snowblaster Incident.”

And yes, if you want to talk to emergency responders in snowy states, they see this every winter. People trying to use totally wrong tools, doing things that make them almost get hurt, and living to regret it. They put up warnings about snowblowers jammed with snow, leaf blowers being used as snowblowers, and all kinds of other genius inventions that very clearly belong in a museum of bad ideas.

So let this be the lesson: winter is cold. Snow is heavy. Leaf blowers are for leaves. And if you ever find yourself lying on your back in snow wondering how your life got to this point, maybe consider that there might have been a better plan.

And world peace aside, that right there is one of winter’s greatest gifts.
