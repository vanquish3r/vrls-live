Darwin Award

Alright, gather around, because this is a birthday story that perfectly combines human stupidity with the idea that maybe some traditions are a little too dangerous. So there was this guy—let’s call him “Birthday Boy”—who, like a normal person, probably thought birthdays were about cake, presents, and maybe a few laughs at your own expense. But apparently, in his world, “a few laughs” meant shoving your own face right into the cake. Nose first, frosting everywhere, the whole embarrassing spectacle. Classic birthday fun, right? Who doesn’t love a little chaos?

Except, and this is where it gets deliciously ridiculous, there was a hidden danger in that cake. A sneaky little plastic piece hiding like a ninja in plain sight. Maybe it was part of the decoration. Maybe it was a forgotten packaging shard from some gadget gift. Nobody knows. Nobody cared. Everyone was too busy laughing, filming, or just being mesmerized by the sheer audacity of smashing someone’s face into a confectionary death trap. And Birthday Boy? He didn’t even notice. Nope. He went about his day, frosting in his hair, joy in his heart, and a tiny piece of plastic chilling in his eyelid like it was on vacation.

Now, fast forward eighteen months. That’s right, a year and a half. Because this plastic decided to make its dramatic debut. Swelling, pain, and general “what the hell is happening to me?” symptoms appeared. Enter the emergency room, enter surgery, enter a lot of exasperated doctors probably muttering “people are ridiculous.” And the kicker? This whole fiasco was self-inflicted. He literally did it to himself. The tradition that’s supposed to be fun and celebratory nearly became a minor medical horror story.

---

So, what’s the moral here? Don’t let anyone shove your face into cake. Always check for hidden sharp objects. And maybe, just maybe, think twice before trusting that birthdays are inherently safe. Because apparently, in some circles, they’re like a combination of obstacle course and extreme sport, complete with sneaky hazards and long-term consequences.

And that, my friends, is why Birthday Boy earns an honourable mention in the Darwin Awards of “what were you thinking?” Not because he died. Not because he even came close. But because he survived his own stupidity and gave the rest of us a story to laugh at while quietly questioning the sanity of humanity. Here’s to you, Birthday Boy. May your future birthdays involve cake, laughter, and maybe a tiny bit less risk of embedding foreign objects in your face.
