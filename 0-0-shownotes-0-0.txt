Bright Spot

Tonight I want to highlight someone who brings a really unique kind of magic into this community, and that’s CrypticWoodworker.

Some people show up with words. Some people show up with energy. And then there are people like him who quite literally create things that people can hold, use, and keep. He’s incredibly skilled with woodworking, building everything from puzzle boxes to furniture to fences, the kind of work that takes patience, precision, and a whole lot of dedication. And as if that wasn’t enough, he also does 3D printing, turning ideas into real, tangible objects.

And the best part is, he doesn’t just keep that talent to himself. He’s made things for people in this community and sent them out just because he wanted to. No big reason, no expectation, just wanting to do something kind for someone else. In a space where most of what we share is digital and disappears the second you log off, he’s out here making things people can actually hold onto. That sticks with people.

He’s also just a genuinely great presence to have around. He shows up. He hangs out. He joins in. He’s supportive, easy to be around, and someone who adds to the atmosphere just by being there. You don’t have to guess if he cares, he shows it.

And you can tell he’s someone who works hard in every part of his life. Whether it’s the time and effort he puts into his craft, the care he has for his wife and his pets, or the way he shows up for others, there’s a level of consistency there that really stands out.
---
There’s something grounding about people like him. People who build. People who create. People who take raw materials and turn them into something meaningful. It’s not just about the end result, it’s about the intention behind it.

CrypticWoodworker, you bring something special to this community. Not just your talent, but your generosity, your presence, and the way you show up for people. You make things that last, and in a lot of ways, so does the impact you have here.

We’re lucky to have you with us.

Darwin Award

420 is supposed to be simple. You relax, you chill, maybe you eat everything in your kitchen and then stare into the fridge like it personally betrayed you. It is not supposed to turn into a home chemistry experiment, and yet every year, someone decides they’re not just going to enjoy weed, they’re going to engineer it.

So this guy decides he’s going to make his own cannabis concentrate at home using butane. For anyone who doesn’t know, that process involves blasting butane through plant material to extract THC. For anyone who does know, that sentence already sounds like something that should not be happening indoors, near furniture, in a building that other people also live in.

Because butane isn’t just part of the process, it’s a highly flammable gas. It doesn’t stay politely in one spot. It spreads, it fills the air, and it quietly turns the room into something that is just waiting for a bad decision to finish the job. And that’s the part people seem to underestimate, especially when confidence is high and judgment is… on vacation.

So he’s inside, windows closed, fully committed to his DIY setup like he’s starring in a show called Breaking Bad Decisions. The butane is building up, everything feels fine, maybe even productive. Meanwhile, the room is essentially becoming a bomb with furniture.

All it takes is one spark. A pilot light, a fridge kicking on, static electricity, flipping a switch. Something small, something normal, something that on any other day would mean absolutely nothing. And then suddenly, it means everything.
---
The whole place ignites in a flash. Explosion, fire, windows blowing out, the kind of moment where every neighbor instantly stops what they’re doing and goes, “yeah, that’s not good.” Fire departments have responded to situations exactly like this. Apartments destroyed, buildings evacuated, people injured, all because someone thought their living room was a safe place to run a flammable gas experiment.

And somehow, some of them survive. Burned, shaken, and very quickly brought back to reality, standing outside watching their building like, “I might have made a mistake.” You can just imagine the conversation when firefighters show up and immediately piece it together. There’s always that pause where no one even knows where to start, because the explanation is somehow worse than the damage.

The thing is, 420 is supposed to lower stress, not remove your eyebrows. You don’t need a lab setup, flammable gas, and a dream. You need a couch, some snacks, and maybe the ability to not accidentally create a headline.

So if your plan involves butane, poor ventilation, and a level of confidence that doesn’t match the situation, it might be time to rethink things. Because the goal of 420 is to get a little elevated, not to take your entire apartment with you.
