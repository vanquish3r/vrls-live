The Customer Is Not Always Right

A rude customer interacts with a waitress or hostess who remains professionally polite on the outside—while their uncensored inner thoughts say everything service workers have ever wanted to say out loud.

Each beat follows this rhythm:
    
* Customer says something rude
* Waitress responds professionally
* Inner voice escalates with sharper honesty

Opening - customer enters/is greeted etc. Customer is already irritable and passive aggressive.

Escalation - going through normal rude customer behavior including complaining about wait times, prices, saying they know the owner, asking for the impossible, interrupting, being rude - etc etc

Almost snap - customer crosses a line, innervoice says what everyone wishes they could say in that scenario, outer voice is still calm and professional.

Ending - Customer storms out swearing they'll never come back again.

Darwin Award 

A man decided to surprise his kids on Christmas Eve by dressing up as Santa and entering the house through the chimney. Not metaphorically. Literally.

Important context. This was a modern house. The fireplace had been converted years earlier. The chimney was decorative. It had a metal flue liner, a damper that did not fully open, and absolutely no business being used as a human entry point.

But Santa logic kicked in.

He waited until late at night, put on a full Santa suit, including the beard, and climbed onto the roof carrying a sack of presents. His plan was to slide down, pop out of the fireplace, ho ho ho, core memory unlocked.

Instead, he got stuck.

Not “oh no my foot is wedged.” Fully stuck. Shoulders pinned. Arms trapped. Beard jammed in the flue. He could not move up or down. Gravity had him. Physics had him. Christmas had him in a chokehold.

He was upside down long enough that blood started rushing to his head. He began to panic. Panic makes you breathe faster. Fast breathing inside a tight metal chimney is not ideal. The soot and dust kicked up, making it harder to breathe. His heart rate spiked. He started yelling.

---

His kids woke up to what they later described as “Santa screaming inside the walls.”

At first, the family thought it was a prank. Then they realised the voice was getting weaker.

Emergency services were called. Firefighters arrived to find a man dressed as Santa trapped upside down in a chimney, turning purple, and still clutching a bag of presents like commitment would somehow fix this.

They had to partially dismantle the fireplace from the living room side. This involved power tools, debris everywhere, and firefighters trying very hard not to laugh while also trying to keep him alive.

After an extremely uncomfortable rescue, he was pulled out, dehydrated, bruised, and suffering from circulation issues from being inverted for so long. He was taken to hospital and treated. He survived.

Doctors later said that if it had gone on much longer, he could have lost consciousness and died from positional asphyxiation or complications from blood pooling in his head.

The kids still got their presents. Christmas technically happened. But now their family holiday lore includes the year Santa almost died in the chimney because Dad did not understand basic home construction.

Fire departments now regularly warn people not to attempt Santa chimney entrances, which feels like a sentence that should not need to exist.

It is, however, a reminder that Christmas magic does not override physics, and if your house was built after about 1950, the chimney is not a slide.
