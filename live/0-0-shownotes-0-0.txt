You ever work for someone who thinks they know everything but somehow manages to mess up literally everything they touch? That’s my boss. Every shift with him feels like an interactive tutorial on “How Not to Restaurant.”

One day he’s talking to me, trying to look all in charge, and mid-sentence he drops actual silverware straight into a takeout bag. Like full-on fork, knife, spoon. I’m watching it happen like, “Ah yes, nothing says ‘to-go order’ like cutlery that could stab a small child.”

I tell him, “You just put silverware in that bag.”
He looks at me completely serious and says, “No, I didn’t.”
Buddy… it’s clinking. The bag sounds like a wind chime in a hurricane.

Later he tells me to pack up the drive-thru orders, so I grab all the food and all the chits. He immediately goes, “If you were gonna do that, I would’ve bagged it myself. You’re confusing me. You’re going to get confused.”

I look him dead in the eyes and say, “I’m not confused.” Then I walk away like I just solved world peace… for about four seconds until he forgets to put a sandwich on an order.

He slows everyone down so bad ticket times double, he forgets stuff on plates, forgets items in takeout bags. And when people call back to complain, he’s all, “I don’t know how that happened.”
Yeah you do, Sherlock. You happened.

So yeah, if you ever wonder how the food comes out wrong… just remember, it’s not you. It’s him. And somehow we all survive to serve another order.

You ever have a customer that just… exists to ruin your day? I had this one, let’s call her Methany, because the vibes were strong. She was a semi-regular, always weird, always dramatic. One day she comes in, gets her order, leaves, and then comes back swearing we forgot part of it.

I bagged that order myself. I checked off every single item on the receipt as I packed it. I know damn well nothing was missing. But Methany was convinced I was running some kind of restaurant conspiracy.

So I go to the back to “view the footage,” which, in my world, meant calling the police, because she’s yelling, throwing things, and some other idiot customer decided they were going to defend her… like I needed backup from the peanut gallery.

Methany starts grabbing stuff off the counter and hurling it, threatening to call the police on us. I just calmly tell her, “Yeah, the police are on their way, by the way.” She disappears. For a few days. I think, finally, peace.

Then she comes back to “apologize.” Turns out she found the missing item… on the floor of her car.

I never served her again. And honestly, she was just the cherry on top of the daily chaos sundae. Between a boss who forgets sandwiches, puts silverware in takeout bags, and customers who think screaming makes food taste better, Methany was basically a training exercise in human insanity.

And the best part? You earned your survival badge. You earned your emergency chocolate ration. Congratulations, you leveled up in human chaos… and unlocked the “Why am I still here?” achievement.

Dear kitty: Terrible Advice for Terrible People

Welcome to Dear kitty, the advice column where people send me their problems and I make them significantly worse.

Now, I should probably mention that I am not a licensed therapist, relationship counsellor, or qualified professional of any kind. I'm just a woman with a microphone, an opinion, and absolutely no business giving anybody life advice.

So, with that in mind, let's see whose life we're ruining today!

**DAN:** (FEMALE)

Dear Kitty,

My boyfriend spends sixteen hours a day gaming. He can build an entire automated chicken farm in Minecraft, but somehow he doesn't know how to load the dishwasher.

Yesterday he told me he wasn't sure where the clean plates went. We've lived together for seven years. How do I get him to help around the house?

Sincerely, Living with a Legendary Idiot.

**KITTY:**

Dear Legendary Idiot's Girlfriend,

Your boyfriend has mastered electricity, engineering, resource management, and the reproductive habits of digital livestock.

I promise you, the man understands plates. Here's what you're going to do. Turn the household chores into an RPG.

Leave little quest markers around the house. Every time he picks up a sock, he gets five experience points. Every time he washes a dish, he unlocks a new achievement.

"Congratulations! You've unlocked BASIC HUMAN FUNCTIONING!"

And when he finally cleans the bathroom, you can reward him with the rarest achievement of all. A girlfriend who doesn't want to murder him.

**DAN:** (EITHER)

Dear Kitty,

I have a friend who refuses to text. Instead, he sends me voice messages. Not short ones, either. I'm talking eight, nine, sometimes twelve minutes long.

Half the message is him forgetting what he was talking about. Last week I listened to him make a sandwich, yell at his dog, and apparently take a piss. When I ask him to just text me, he says I'm not appreciating authentic communication. What should I do?

Sincerely, Trapped in a Podcast.

**KITTY:**

Dear Trapped,

You don't have a friend. You have a podcast subscription you never agreed to. It's time to fight fire with fire. The next time he sends you a twelve-minute voice message, respond with a forty-minute recording of your washing machine.

No explanation. No introduction. Just forty uninterrupted minutes of the spin cycle. If he complains, tell him it's an immersive audio experience about your emotional journey through fabric softener. And if that doesn't work, start sending him dramatic readings of your grocery receipts.

"One cucumber. Two dollars and forty-nine cents. WHY HATH YOU FORSAKEN ME, SOBEYS?!" He'll learn to text.

**DAN:** (EITHER)

Dear Kitty,

I met the most amazing man in VR. He's charming, romantic, and tells me I'm his soulmate. He says he owns six properties, he's financially independent, and he's an entrepreneur.

Unfortunately, all six properties are virtual worlds, his business is selling avatar textures, and when I asked where he lives, he said, "Physically or spiritually?"

We've been together three months, and I've still never seen his face. Am I being too suspicious?

Sincerely, Virtually in Love.

**KITTY:**

Dear Virtually in Love,

First of all, congratulations on your relationship with what might be three raccoons operating a headset. I'm sure his six properties are lovely, but unless the bank has started accepting Unity assets as collateral, I'm not sure you're marrying into money.

Now, there's nothing wrong with meeting someone in VR. Some amazing relationships have started that way. But there's mysterious, and then there's "I'm dating a man whose entire legal identity is a purple dragon named DaddyThunder69."

Here's my suggestion. Ask him one very simple question. "What do you do when your headset battery dies?" If he says, "Usually I go upstairs and spend some time with my wife and kids," congratulations!

You've just discovered his seventh property. The doghouse he's about to be living in.

**DAN:** (FEMALE)

Dear Kitty,

My friend is getting married in VR and asked me to be her maid of honour. I thought that sounded fun.

Now she wants me to organize six rehearsals, coordinate thirty guests, arrange the music, settle an argument between her and the groom's ex, and somehow build an entire wedding chapel.

Yesterday she threatened to replace me because I didn't like her choice of virtual flowers. Am I a bad friend for wanting to quit?

Sincerely, Bridesmaid.exe Has Stopped Working.

**KITTY:**

Dear Bridesmaid,

Let me get this straight.

You're planning a wedding, mediating relationship disputes, managing thirty people, and doing virtual construction work? For free?

Sweetheart, you're not a maid of honour. You're an unpaid wedding planner with Stockholm syndrome. Here's what you need to do. Send the bride an invoice. Include separate charges for event management, emotional damages, and emergency bullshit removal.

Add an extra five hundred dollars for every time you've heard the words "It's MY special day!" And if she threatens to replace you again, tell her that's perfectly fine. You wish her and her future ex-husband a lifetime of virtual happiness. Or at least until someone forgets to renew the server.

**DAN:** (EITHER)

Dear Kitty,

My girlfriend says I have a problem with VR. Every night I promise I'll log off by eleven, but somehow it's four in the morning before I finally get to bed. Last night I spent forty-five minutes saying goodbye to the same five people.

I know I should probably cut back, but I really don't think I have a problem. What do you think?

Sincerely, Just Five More Minutes.

**KITTY:**

Dear Five More Minutes,

I think you have a problem. But not the one you think. Your problem is that you apparently believe every time you leave a virtual room, you're departing on the Titanic. You don't need to personally address every single passenger.

You don't need to tell people how much they mean to you. You don't need one final hug. And you certainly don't need to announce your departure, then spend another two hours discussing the departure.

Here's what you do. At eleven o'clock, say, "Goodnight, everybody!" And then... This is the important part...

TURN THE HEADSET OFF!

I know. Revolutionary concept. If you absolutely must make a big emotional farewell, record one in advance and play it every night. Include some sad violin music. Maybe a slideshow. A few photographs of the good times.

And remember, these people are not witnessing your final moments on Earth. You're going to see them again tomorrow. Probably at eight in the morning, because none of you have gone to bed.

**KITTY:**

Well, that's all the terrible advice we have time for tonight.

Remember, if you're having relationship problems, friendship problems, or just generally making questionable life choices, Dear Kitty is here to help.

**DAN:**

Or make things worse. There's really no quality control.

Until next time, make good choices. Or don't.

Missed Connections: The Unhinged Edition

**KITTY (opening):**
You know, sometimes love finds us in the most unexpected places.

At a party. In a nightclub. Standing in line for a coffee. Or in a virtual world, where the person you've fallen desperately in love with might actually be a forty-seven-year-old man sitting in his underwear eating Doritos.

**PANDA:**
Tonight, we're exploring the beautiful, romantic, and occasionally deeply disturbing world of missed connections.

These are the stories of people who almost found love.

And after hearing them, I think you'll understand why they probably didn't.

**KITTY:**
To the handsome man in the tuxedo standing near the virtual buffet last Saturday...

You were looking incredibly sophisticated. I was the woman in the red dress attempting to eat a block of cheese without taking off my headset.

When my microphone accidentally picked up the sound of me chewing, you said, "Is somebody eating?"

I said, "Somebody's living." You laughed. I laughed. And then my headset fell directly into the cheese. I haven't stopped thinking about you. If you're out there, please get in touch. I'll bring the crackers.

**PANDA**
So let me get this straight. She was aggressively eating cheese, making questionable noises into her microphone, and ended up face-first in a block of cheddar? Somebody needs an adult...

**KITTY:**
To the beautiful woman I met on the dance floor...

You said you loved how mysterious and quiet I was. The truth is, my microphone was broken. You said you loved how I stood completely still and just watched you. My controllers were dead.

And when you told me you'd never met someone with such an intense, smouldering stare... My headset had frozen. I wanted to tell you how I felt, but my battery died.

Please find me. You're the only person who's ever mistaken catastrophic hardware failure for sex appeal.

**PANDA:** 
He couldn't speak, couldn't move, and ran out of juice after five minutes? That's not your dream man. That's a vibrator with a warranty issue.

**KITTY:**
To the man with the impossibly deep voice at the party last night...

You walked into the room and simply said, "Testing. One. Two." At that exact moment, six women stopped talking. Three changed their relationship status. One audibly whispered, "Daddy?"

I don't know your name. I don't know what you look like. I don't even know if you're single. But I do know that when you said, "Can everybody hear me?" I nearly proposed. Please contact me.

**PANDA**
Ladies, the man was just testing his microphone!

At this point, he could read the terms and conditions of a software update and half the room would be moaning, "Don't stop, I'm almost there!"

**KITTY:**
To the beautiful woman in the front row of my open mic performance...

I could tell you were captivated by my poetry. When I began my fourteen-minute spoken-word piece about my divorce and the emotional symbolism of a broken ethernet cable, you seemed deeply moved.

When I reached the section where I compared my ex-wife to an outdated router, you began shaking your head. I know. Powerful stuff. And when you finally stood up and walked away, I could see the emotions had become overwhelming.

If you're reading this, I would love to perform the extended twenty-seven-minute version for you.

**PANDA:**
Sir. She wasn't overwhelmed by emotion. She was looking for the exit. And I'm pretty sure the broken ethernet cable was trying to leave too.

**KITTY:**
To the mysterious woman I met beneath the virtual moon...

You told me you were an eight-hundred-year-old demon queen, ruler of the underworld, and destroyer of mortal souls. I was intimidated. But also strangely aroused.

We danced beneath the stars. You spoke of ancient prophecies. You promised me immortality. Then your husband walked into the room and yelled, "Linda! Did you remember to put the chicken in the air fryer?"

And you screamed, "GO AWAY, GARY, I'M BUSY!"

Linda... If you're reading this, I don't care that you're not really a demon queen. Any woman who can command the armies of hell and make crispy potatoes is the woman for me.

**PANDA:**
I'm rooting for these two. Although I'm a little concerned about Gary.

**KITTY:**
To the man whose microphone was accidentally open at two in the morning...

I heard everything. I heard you put bread in the toaster. I heard you wait. And then I heard you scream, "YOU BURNT THE BREAD AGAIN, YOU USELESS PIECE OF CRAP!"

You proceeded to have a full-blown argument with your toaster. You accused it of betraying you. You demanded an apology. At one point, you threatened to replace it with an air fryer.

I've never witnessed a man so passionate about breakfast. If you're single, please reach out. I want someone who fights that hard for the things he loves.

**PANDA:**
If that's how he reacts when something gets a little too hot, I'd be careful. The man can't even handle two minutes of heat without losing his mind.

**KITTY:**
To the handsome stranger sitting alone at the nightclub...

I swear I wasn't trying to sit on your lap. My playspace moved. One moment I was dancing, and the next my avatar was straddling your face. I panicked. You panicked.

I tried to stand up, but my tracking glitched, and somehow I ended up upside down with my legs sticking through your head. You whispered, "This is the most action I've had in six months."

And I knew. I knew right then. You were the one. Please find me. I've since recalibrated my tracking, but I'm willing to break it again for you.

**PANDA**
You know, most people meet their soulmate through mutual friends, dating apps, or shared interests. This woman met hers through a series of technical failures that would normally require a support ticket.

**KITTY:**
To the kind, patient stranger who spent three hours helping me fix my avatar... You walked me through every step. You never raised your voice. You didn't laugh when I accidentally deleted the entire project.

You calmly explained what I needed to do, even when I asked the same question seventeen times. And when I finally got everything working, you simply said, "Glad I could help," and disappeared into the night.

I don't know if you're single. I don't even know your real name. But I am prepared to marry you. I've never met a person who could troubleshoot Unity without making me feel like a complete idiot.

Please find me. Our children will be beautiful. And hopefully know how to read error messages.

**PANDA:**
Forget flowers and chocolates. The hottest thing a man can say these days is, "Don't worry, I've already fixed it."


**KITTY:**
To the person I met in the original Altspace all those years ago...

You said you were going to the bathroom and you'd be right back. I believed you. I waited. The party ended. The world emptied. The platform eventually shut down. Years passed.

People moved on. Entire communities rebuilt themselves. And now, Altspace has returned. So I'm writing this in the hopes that you'll finally see it. WHERE THE HELL ARE YOU? DID YOU FALL IN?

**PANDA:**
Either this person has the worst case of constipation in human history... Or they figured out how to leave a party without spending forty-five minutes saying goodbye to everyone.

**KITTY:**
To the handsome stranger who pretended to be my boyfriend at a party last Saturday...

You have no idea how grateful I am. He'd trapped me for forty-five minutes mansplaining feminism to me. At one point, he actually said, "You women would get a lot further if you'd just listen to us."

I was desperate. So when I saw you standing nearby, I ran over, grabbed your arm, and shouted, "BABE! THERE YOU ARE!" Without missing a beat, you wrapped your arm around me and said, "Honey, we need to go. The babysitter just called."

You looked me dead in the eyes and said, "Jimothy's fine. It's the twins we're worried about." THE TWINS?!

By the time we escaped, we'd apparently been married twelve years, had three children, and were in couples counselling because you couldn't keep your hands off the neighbour.

Please find me.

**PANDA**
